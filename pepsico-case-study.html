PepsiCo's 2025 and 2026 narrative is one of bold innovation - the poppi acquisition, Siete Foods, Pepsi Prebiotic, Doritos Protein. On the surface, a company pivoting hard toward Edge and Beyond. But when you run the Theta framework, it's a double down on Core with a starved Edge.

Theta has two diagnostic questions: how far from existing capabilities (tech complexity), and how far from existing customers (market reach). Poppi is a functional beverage. PepsiCo already owns beverages, distribution, and the shelf. The buyer is the same, the channel is the same, which makes it an Adjacent Innovation under the Core archetype. Siete is a grain-free snack brand. PepsiCo already owns Frito-Lay, the same distribution, and the same shelf. Adjacent Innovation again. Pepsi Prebiotic is a new variant of an existing brand in an existing category. Core Innovation. Doritos Protein is a reformulation. Core Innovation.

The only genuine Edge move in the portfolio is SodaStream Professional. The same carbonation hardware redirected into B2B offices and hospitality, an entirely new customer segment. But one Architectural Innovation does not make PepsiCo an Edge driven company.

This matters because Elliott Management, the same activist that ran this playbook at PayPal, is now holding the pen at PepsiCo. At PayPal, the result was Core-maximization that starved the Edge and Beyond until there was nothing behind the Core when it softened. At PepsiCo the mechanism is subtler. Not cuts, but reclassification. Adjacent moves count as Edge, which lets the narrative sound bold while the actual allocation stays conservative.

Nooyi's era was dismissed by some as unfocused. It turns out to have been PepsiCo's most balanced allocation. The activist era did not expand the frontier. It rebranded the Core.

The Company

A distribution empire
with brands as cover.

PepsiCo is not a food and beverage company. It is one of the most sophisticated distribution networks ever assembled, and the brands are what make retailers and consumers want to use it. When Pepsi-Cola merged with Frito-Lay in 1965, the combined entity did something no competitor could easily replicate: it put the drink in your hand and the chip on your tongue in the same truck, from the same relationship, on the same shelf. Today 23 brands each generate over one billion dollars annually, and most consumers have no idea they share a parent.

That invisibility is intentional. PepsiCo operates closer to LVMH than to Coca-Cola, a House of Brands where each brand is sovereign and the corporate name recedes. The model generates enormous resilience. It also generates enormous complexity, which is exactly what an activist investor targets first.

Something most people don't know

Pepsi-Cola was formulated in 1898 as a digestive tonic, not a soft drink. Its name derives from "dyspepsia." The original slogan was "Exhilarating, Invigorating, Aids Digestion." Over 125 years later, the company's most ambitious bets are in prebiotics and gut health. It did not evolve into wellness. It returned to where it began, this time with the science to prove it.

Performance with Purpose: the most balanced era

Nooyi arrived seeing a curve no one else wanted to call flat. Soda volumes were slowing; obesity was becoming political. She introduced "Performance with Purpose" and meant it structurally: reduce sugar and sodium, acquire genuine challengers in health and wellness, invest in sustainability infrastructure. The Adjacent turn: called innovation, mapped as Core

The 2025 reset is operationally coherent: 20% of the product lineup cut, three plants closed, savings reinvested into targeted acquisitions. poppi for $1.95B, Siete for $1.2B, Pepsi Prebiotic launched, Doritos Protein rolled out. The narrative frames these as Edge innovation. The Theta map disagrees. Every one of these sits in the Core zone: same shelf, same distribution, same buyer, same capability. The one genuine Edge move is SodaStream Professional. The Beyond is structurally unfunded, measured by Core KPIs, and has no named champion in the organization.

What the Theta Framework Asks

Questions that a press release
cannot answer.

01
"If poppi and Siete are Adjacent Innovation sitting in the Core zone, what is PepsiCo's actual Edge portfolio right now?"
This is not a rhetorical question. An honest answer changes the entire read of the company's innovation health, and the allocation decisions that follow.
02
"Who at PepsiCo is employed to think about 2038, what is their reporting line, and what is their budget?"
The Beyond zone requires a named champion with protected funding and a timeline insulated from quarterly review. If the answer is "no one specifically," that is the diagnostic. Structural problems require structural answers.
03
"Nestlé is running clinical trials on biological ageing and gut microbiome science. Is PepsiCo's absence from that space a deliberate strategy, or an oversight?"
The distinction matters. A deliberate choice to let Nestlé own the science while PepsiCo owns the distribution is coherent. An oversight is a vulnerability. The responses are entirely different.
04
"What would a successful intelligent failure in the Beyond zone look like at PepsiCo, and when did we last have one?"
Low failure rates in an innovation portfolio are not a sign of excellence. They are often a sign that the team has stopped taking risks worth failing at. If PepsiCo cannot name one, the Beyond zone is not being used. It is being performed.
05
"After the 20% SKU cut, how many of the remaining products are genuinely differentiated, and how many are simply the ones we didn't have the courage to remove?"
Simplification is not the same as focus. The question is whether the remaining portfolio represents PepsiCo's best thinking about where consumers are going, or its most defensible thinking about where they have been.

Risk and Failure Tolerance

The 20% SKU cut and plant closures signal a culture that rewards ROI discipline over experimentation. PepsiCo's Beyond zone shows no visible intelligent failures: no experiments that failed publicly, were absorbed, and changed strategy. Per the Theta framework, that is not a clean record. It is a warning sign.

Customer Signal Alignment

PepsiCo listens to core customers through scanner data, loyalty programs, and retail feedback with precision. It listens to early adopters by acquiring brands they have already validated: poppi proved the prebiotic soda market before PepsiCo entered it. The gap is the absence of a systematized signal operation. The company buys signals rather than building the antenna.

Relativity Assessment

In mature CPG, transformation looks different than in tech. A 78% Core allocation is not inherently wrong. Gerstner's near-zero Beyond was the correct move for a dying IBM. The question for PepsiCo is whether the Core consolidation is a deliberate strategic posture or a default drift, and whether the company can distinguish between the two before the next curve arrives without them on it.
