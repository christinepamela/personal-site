Mee Goreng Mamak

The one the grandchildren ask for on Sunday mornings.

Noodles Serves 4 25 minutes One wok

“Yellow noodles, not spaghetti — the yellow ones hold the sauce. If I have prawns I put prawns, if not, chicken also can. The secret is the potato and the tofu, fried first so they soak up the gravy, and the egg broken in right at the end.”

Ingredients

The noodles

  • 450 gYellow egg noodles — rinsed, drained well
  • 2Potatoes — boiled, cut into cubes, fried golden
  • 2 piecesFirm tofu — cubed and fried
  • 3Eggs
  • 150 gPrawns or chicken — either one
  • 1 handfulBean sprouts

The spice paste

  • 5Dried chillies — soaked in hot water
  • 4Shallots
  • 3 clovesGarlic
  • 2 cmGinger
  • 1 tbspCurry powder — the meat curry powder, not fish
  • 1 tspBelacan — toasted first

To finish

  • 3 tbspSweet soy sauce — kecap manis
  • 2 tbspTomato ketchup
  • 1Tomato — cut into wedges
  • 1Lime
  • to tasteSalt and sugar

Method

  1. Pound the chillies, shallots, garlic, ginger and belacan into a paste. Stir the curry powder into it with a splash of water so it does not burn.
  2. Fry the potato cubes and tofu in oil until golden. Take them out and keep them aside — they go back in at the end so they stay whole.
  3. In the same wok, fry the spice paste on medium heat until the oil splits and it smells cooked, about 5 minutes. Do not rush this part.
  4. Add the prawns or chicken and stir until just cooked, then push everything to one side of the wok.
  5. Break in the eggs and let them set for a moment before scrambling them through.
  6. Add the noodles, sweet soy sauce, ketchup, tomato wedges, salt and a pinch of sugar. Toss with two spatulas or chopsticks — the noodles should be coated, not swimming.
  7. Return the potato and tofu, throw in the bean sprouts, and toss for one more minute. The sprouts should still squeak.
  8. Squeeze the lime over the top and serve straight away, with sliced green chilli on the side if anyone wants it hotter.
Mom’s note If the noodles look dry, add a spoon of water — not oil. Water makes the sauce cling; oil makes it greasy.

Where this comes from

Mamak food is the Indian-Muslim cooking of Malaysia — Tamil kitchens that took on local chillies, belacan and sweet soy sauce. In our house it picked up a Chinese touch too (the ketchup, the bean sprouts), which is exactly why it tastes like home and not like a restaurant.
