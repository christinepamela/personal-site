Snap Inc Theta Case Study
The Theta Lens  ·  Snap Inc

A company with a Core and a Beyond, and a missing Edge

Snap’s existential crisis lies in the disconnect between how it views its internal roadmap vs how it is actually positioned on the Theta map. The Edge that should grow into the next Core has been bought, absorbed or shut down.

How to read this page

Three zones, one founder holding the pen

Theta sorts innovation by technological complexity and market reach into Core (extend what already sells), Edge (new products and markets that can grow into the next Core) and Beyond (long-horizon bets that rebuild the company's footing). The book's warning about companies with only Core and Beyond is blunt: when the Core slows, there is nothing to take over.

Part one · the illusion of the Edge

Two maps of the same company

Snap describes Stories, Spotlight, Snap Map and Lenses as new bets that grew out of the camera. Read on Theta's axes they are extensions of one product, sold to the same users, funded by the same ad engine. The Edge that was once real is a list of things that no longer exist.

The Edge was not neglected. It was pruned.

Snap ran acquired ventures on their own as Edge bets. Zenly stayed a separate app, with a reported 40 million monthly users but Snap shut it in August 2022. Pixy, Originals, Minis and Games, and Voisey went the same way. Snap tried, but it had limited patience or vision to establish new Cores. What survived was what could be re-directed into the exact same Core.

Camera Kit, the AR Enterprise suite (ARES) and Lens monetisation began as outward-facing, separate-looking bets. They now sit inside the Core as extensions of Lenses and ad sales. An Edge innovation has the potential to become a new Core. At Snap, an Edge idea either becomes a Core feature or it is closed.

Part three · who holds the pen

The Beyond survives because the vote does not move

Snap's public Class A shares carry no votes. The founders hold roughly 97% of the voting power. In a company with ordinary governance, a Beyond line burning about $500M a year, and more than $3.5B cumulatively, would be a standing target. Snap reports a GAAP net loss ($164M in Q2 2026) while generating positive free cash flow, so the AR bill is paid from the Core.

Activists chop the Edge. Here the founder holds the pen, and the founder has chopped it himself.

The book names the usual pattern: activists and financial desperation cut the Edge first, because it earns nothing yet. Snap inverts it. Irenic and Blue Duck can make noise, but they cannot take control. The risk is inside the structure: one allocator, no outside check on the portfolio's shape, and a Core that the Beyond depends on. This is also the contrast with PayPal's case, where the pen sat with a board that could change hands.

What the structure protects

Long bets nobody can vote down

A decade of AR funding survived a $39.9M write-down, redesigns and a cash-burn bill that outside shareholders would likely have ended.

What the structure cannot protect

An Edge nobody is accountable for

The 2022 closures were a founder's decision made under ad-revenue pressure. No one else was positioned to argue that Zenly's users were a future Core.

Part four · where Snap departs from the framework

Breakthrough, built a different way

The book's route to breakthrough innovation runs through three stages: Chaos with a few mavericks, Growth as a skunkworks that crosses the chasm with early adopters, and Scale, where the breakthrough is opened internally so the existing Core can adapt it. Snap does it differently in four ways.

One more difference sits outside Snap. Meta's glasses strategy is an Edge-to-Core path: a product already selling to a mass audience, extended toward richer displays. Snap's is Beyond-first, with no mass-market glasses revenue underneath. The relativity principle in the book matters here: the same product is Edge for one company and Core for another, depending on where its customers already are.

Part five · the status quo

If nothing changes

These are the author's scenarios, not forecasts, and they carry no probabilities.

Scenario A

Specs finds a niche

Early buyers make AR a paying Beyond. Core ad products gain a new surface.

Theta reading: Beyond matures into a business, but Snap still has no Edge to take over from a slowing camera.

Scenario B

Specs stalls, pressure builds

Sales undershoot, losses continue and activists use the burn to demand cuts.

Theta reading: the structure resists a forced sale. The founder trims the Beyond, and Snap becomes a Core-only company.

Scenario C

A larger platform wins the glasses

Meta, Apple or Google ships a mass-market device first and sets the standard.

Theta reading: Snap's seven-year Beyond becomes a stepping stone for someone else, and the Core is the only asset left.

In all three, the camera business carries the company. The case turns on whether that Core is given something to hand over to.

Part six · the Theta path

Restore the Edge, and build the bridge both ways

Using the book's method means two changes: put an Edge back on the map, and run the AR bet through Chaos, Growth and Scale instead of one long funding line.

  • Rebuild the Edge

    Take the closed ventures seriously as seeds. Zenly's location graph, Minis and Games as a developer platform, and Camera Kit as a business that sells outside Snapchat are three candidates. Give each a protected budget, separate metrics from the Core's, and a stated review date.

  • AR in three stages

    Chaos: a small maverick team per use case. Growth: enterprise and developer early adopters as the chasm crossing, before consumer scale. Scale: open the platform internally so Core ad sales, legal, supply chain and the Lens community co-own what ships.

  • A two-way bridge

    Specs Inc. separates the bet. Theta needs the other half: named people who move from Core to AR and back, and a rule that Core products adopt AR capabilities on a schedule. Without it the separation is only an accounting boundary.

  • A second check on the pen

    The founder's control is the reason the Beyond has lasted. A portfolio review that includes independent directors would keep that protection and answer the question nobody currently asks of the Edge.

Snap shows what a company looks like when it lets the camera absorb every idea and funds the future out of the present. What will the Core hand over to, on the day it slows?
