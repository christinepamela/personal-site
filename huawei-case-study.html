Huawei Through the Theta Lens
The Theta Lens · Case Study · Huawei Technologies

They shut the supply chain. The fire never dimmed.

Huawei had a Plan B when sanctions hit, but that was just the beginning. The real drive was to become a game changer, and no amount of suppression could dim that ambition. This case study tracks thirty-nine years of Huawei’s journey through Core, Edge, and Beyond.

The story in one page

Forced shut, not beaten

In May 2019 Huawei's supply chain was cut by force. Washington called it national security. The author's reading is that it was also a company becoming too strong for the businesses it was overtaking, and a competitor was being removed from the board.

A company in that position usually panics: it cuts Beyond to save Core and sells what it can. Huawei did the reverse, and it did it calmly. It strengthened the Core that paid the bills. It brought out its Plan B, the chips, operating system and software it had built for exactly this day. And it doubled down on Beyond, knowing the payoff would take years, not quarters.

It could stay calm because the goal was never survival. Huawei was all in to be a game changer, and a shut supply chain only changed the route.

How to read this

Three horizons, one company

Theta splits a portfolio into Core (defend and extend today's business), Edge (adjacent bets that open new markets) and Beyond (patient bets on what doesn't yet exist). The question is never "how much innovation" but "is the allocation matched to the company's position?"

Four eras

The allocation moved with the threat

1987 – 2002

Core, bought with survival

Carrier switches and rural China. Ren Zhengfei's "wolf culture" and a huge sales force win the cities from the countryside inward. The early 2000s brought a near-collapse and reported talks to sell the carrier business. The offer did not close.

DRIVER · scarcity forces focus
IMPACT · a customer-led Core worth defending
2003 – 2018

The spare-tire builder

While competing for carrier contracts, Huawei funds a chip unit (HiSilicon), an operating-system effort, and basic research that no quarterly review could justify. Staff understood these as a "spare tire" nobody hoped to use.

DRIVER · paranoia as strategy
IMPACT · Edge and Beyond ready before demand
16 MAY 2019 · THE LINE

Entity List: the tire is used

US export controls cut access to leading chips, Android services and design tools. Revenue falls sharply in 2021 as the consumer phone business is starved. Core shrinks, so Edge and Beyond are pulled forward overnight.

Core ~503020
DRIVER · external shock
IMPACT · Plan B becomes Plan A, in order of urgency
2023 – 2026

Rebuilding on its own stack

The Mate 60 Pro reappears with a domestic 7nm-class chip in 2023. HarmonyOS NEXT drops Android code. Ascend chips and CANN offer a home-grown AI stack. Yinwang takes the Edge into cars. Revenue passes its pre-sanction peak.

Stages

Chaos, Growth, Scale — at once

Chaos

Ascend, Tau, Moye

Small teams, open questions, tolerance for being wrong. Mathematicians and physicists work beside engineers on problems with no deadline.

Growth

Yinwang, HarmonyOS

Proven demand, scaling partners. Partners shoulder the factory floor and brand while Huawei supplies the stack.

Scale

Carrier, devices

Process discipline, global supply chains, thin margins. The cash engine that pays for the other two.

Huawei's chip path (7nm and below)

2019
Designed by HiSilicon · made at TSMC
Now
Domestic foundry (SMIC) + Tau law

Huawei is not trying to beat TSMC on process nodes. It is trying to make the process node less decisive, with the Tau scaling law and LogicFolding aimed at competitive density without the most advanced lithography. Whether that works at scale is still unproven.

Tau law · who is behind it

Announced, now being licensed

Who is behind it (Tau law unveiled 25 May 2026)

Huawei Kirin
First LogicFolding chips expected in next-generation phones this autumn. Internal use, not a partner.
Empyrean
EDA supplier. Unveiled Argus, a 3D IC verification tool it calls a key carrier for the Tau law. A tool supplier, not a confirmed user.

Oct 2026: a multiyear cross-license in which Qualcomm licenses 680+ Huawei patents, including patents tied to the Tau law and LogicFolding. Qualcomm becomes the net payer for the first time. Subject to US regulatory review.

Independent views

Jensen Huang: "a breakthrough for Huawei" but no immediate threat to TSMC. Counterpoint: the techniques have been used globally for years. ICwise: heat density and yield risks. Morningstar: may not be easily replicable.

Strengths and fault lines

What holds, and what could break

Holds

Ownership. Employee-held capital with no activist to force a Core-only quarter.

Rotation. Three rotating chairs make succession a process.

Preparation. Beyond funded in peacetime, so wartime was a switch, not a start.

Fault lines

Founder age. Ren is 81; the culture is partly a person.

Dependence on policy. Part of Edge demand is state-backed self-reliance, not pure market pull.

Ecosystem gravity. HarmonyOS and CANN need developers who may prefer Android and CUDA.

Open numbers

Digital Power revenue is quoted at different figures by different reports. HarmonyOS share in China rests on one source. Ascend market shares are analyst estimates.

Five questions

What a leader should take away

  1. What is your spare tire? If your Core vanished tomorrow, which funded bet would you already hold?
  2. Who pays for Beyond when Core is strong? Huawei's answer was structure, not sentiment.
  3. Can your Edge run on partners? Yinwang shows scale without owning the factory.
  4. Is your Beyond measured by the wrong clock? Unassessed research is a policy, not an oversight.
  5. Does your leadership outlive its founder? Rotation is a design choice made years early.
Verdict

Theta fit: strong, and unusually deliberate

Huawei's allocation tracked its position more closely than most firms manage in calm conditions. Sanctions did not invent its Edge and Beyond; they ended the debate about funding them. The harder test comes now: not survival, but whether the same discipline holds once the pressure that justified it eases.

The variable was not talent or luck. It was when the allocation was made.

Set against Meta, which spent heavily on Beyond after its Core was threatened, Huawei is the inverse: it paid for Beyond while its Core was safe.
